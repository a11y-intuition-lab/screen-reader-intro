VoiceOver on iPhone

Read step 0 before you turn on VoiceOver. It sets up a shortcut, so you can always turn VoiceOver off again.

Step 0: Learn to turn it off before you turn it on

When VoiceOver is on, your phone works differently. One tap selects something and reads it aloud. You tap twice to open or activate what you selected. That is why you set up a shortcut first.

  1. Open Settings, then Accessibility, then Accessibility Shortcut.
  2. Choose VoiceOver.
  3. Come back to this page.

You can now turn VoiceOver on and off by pressing the side button three times quickly. If your phone has a Home button, press that three times instead.

Stuck? Press the side button three times. Or tell Siri: “Turn off VoiceOver”.

Step 1: Turn on VoiceOver

Press the side button three times. You hear a voice, and a black frame appears around whatever VoiceOver has selected.

From now on VoiceOver reads this page to you. To move on, swipe right with one finger, anywhere on the screen. Try it now.

Good. Each swipe right moves to the next thing on the page. Swipe left to go back.

Step 2: Listen and stop

You will hear far more than you need. The most important skill is stopping the speech.

Tap once with two fingers to stop. Tap with two fingers again to continue.

Practise on this paragraph. It is long on purpose. Screen reader users skim by hearing the first few words and moving on if it is not what they are looking for. That is why the most important words should come first in a text, a link and a button. When you hear that a paragraph is not what you need, just swipe on. You don't have to wait until it has been read to the end. Try stopping the speech now, with two fingers.

To read the whole page from the top, swipe up with two fingers. To read from where you are, swipe down with two fingers. Stop with two fingers whenever you like.

VoiceOver scrolls the page as you swipe. To scroll yourself, swipe up or down with three fingers.

Step 3: Explore by touch and activate

You can also drag one finger around the screen. VoiceOver reads whatever you touch. This is a quick way to find things when you know roughly where they are.

When VoiceOver has selected something, double-tap anywhere on the screen to activate it. You don't need to hit the button itself.

Try it on the button below. Swipe to it, or find it with your finger. Then double-tap.

Screen Curtain

Screen Curtain turns the screen black while VoiceOver keeps working. You then have to rely on what you hear, as many screen reader users do. The same gesture turns it on and off: triple-tap with three fingers. If Zoom is on, quadruple-tap with three fingers.

Try it: turn on Screen Curtain, and go back to the button above by swiping left. Activate it. Then turn Screen Curtain off again.

Can't get the screen back? Triple-tap with three fingers again. If that doesn't help, turn off VoiceOver by pressing the side button three times.

Done for now

You can now turn VoiceOver on and off, move back and forth, stop the speech, find things with your finger, activate them and use Screen Curtain.
