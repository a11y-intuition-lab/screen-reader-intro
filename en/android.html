TalkBack on Android

Read step 0 before you turn on TalkBack. It sets up a shortcut, so you can always turn TalkBack off again.

Menu names vary a little between phones, especially on Samsung. If you can't find an option, search for “TalkBack” in Settings.

Step 0: Learn to turn it off before you turn it on

When TalkBack is on, your phone works differently. One tap selects something and reads it aloud. You tap twice to open or activate what you selected. That is why you set up a shortcut first.

  1. Open Settings, then Accessibility, then TalkBack.
  2. Turn on the TalkBack shortcut.
  3. Come back to this page.

You can now turn TalkBack on and off by holding both volume keys for three seconds. The first time, your phone asks whether you want to use the shortcut.

Stuck? Hold both volume keys for three seconds. Or tell Google Assistant: “Turn off TalkBack”.

Step 1: Turn on TalkBack

Hold both volume keys for three seconds. You hear a voice, and a coloured frame appears around whatever TalkBack has selected.

From now on TalkBack reads this page to you. To move on, swipe right with one finger, anywhere on the screen. Try it now.

Good. Each swipe right moves to the next thing on the page. Swipe left to go back.

Tap once with three fingers. If a menu opens, your phone supports multi-finger gestures, which this introduction uses. Close the menu by swiping down then left in one movement. If no menu opens, your phone uses one-finger gestures only. You will find them in the TalkBack tutorial, in TalkBack settings.

Step 2: Listen and stop

You will hear far more than you need. The most important skill is stopping the speech.

Tap once with two fingers to stop. Tap with two fingers again to continue.

Practise on this paragraph. It is long on purpose. Screen reader users skim by hearing the first few words and moving on if it is not what they are looking for. That is why the most important words should come first in a text, a link and a button. When you hear that a paragraph is not what you need, just swipe on. You don't have to wait until it has been read to the end. Try stopping the speech now, with two fingers.

To read the whole page from the top, open the TalkBack menu by tapping with three fingers, and choose “Read from top”. Stop with two fingers whenever you like.

TalkBack scrolls the page as you swipe. To scroll yourself, swipe up or down with two fingers.

Step 3: Explore by touch and activate

You can also drag one finger around the screen. TalkBack reads whatever you touch. This is a quick way to find things when you know roughly where they are.

When TalkBack has selected something, double-tap anywhere on the screen to activate it. You don't need to hit the button itself.

Try it on the button below. Swipe to it, or find it with your finger. Then double-tap.

Hide screen

TalkBack can turn the screen black while TalkBack keeps working. You then have to rely on what you hear, as many screen reader users do. Open the TalkBack menu by tapping with three fingers, and choose “Hide screen”. Choose the same option in the menu to show the screen again.

Try it: hide the screen, and go back to the button above by swiping left. Activate it. Then show the screen again.

Can't get the screen back? Open the TalkBack menu with three fingers and choose to show the screen. If that doesn't help, turn off TalkBack by holding both volume keys.

Done for now

You can now turn TalkBack on and off, move back and forth, stop the speech, find things with your finger, activate them and hide the screen.
